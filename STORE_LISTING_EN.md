**Title (max 30):** MetaCheck: EXIF & GPS Remover

**Short description (max 80):**
See what your photos reveal: GPS, camera, author. Remove it in one tap.

**Full description (max 4000):**
A phone photo carries more than you see: the exact place it was taken, the phone model, the camera serial number, the author's name, the time.
MetaCheck shows what your picture reveals and removes it before you send it.

Photo privacy:
• Privacy score 0–100 and a list of what the recipient will see: coordinates, serial number, author, capture time.
• "Send without metadata" in the Share menu: pick it in your gallery or any app and the photo goes out already cleaned.
• Metadata cleaning: a copy without EXIF, GPS and XMP; the original file is not changed. GPS is removed from JPEG without re-compression.
• Finds where the photo was taken and, when you press the button, shows the point on a map.

All metadata:
• EXIF: camera, lens, shutter speed, aperture, ISO, dates and more, with field search.
• XMP, IPTC and PNG text chunks; export the report as PDF, JSON and CSV.
• Edit EXIF (date, camera, author, coordinates) in a JPEG copy.

AI labels in the file:
• Content Credentials (C2PA): "created by AI" and "edited with AI" labels from ChatGPT, Gemini, Adobe Firefly and others.
• IPTC labels, Stable Diffusion, ComfyUI and NovelAI generation parameters, and about 70 generator names.
• Important: only labels inside the file are checked; the picture itself is not analyzed. Social networks and messengers strip labels,
  so for such images the app honestly says there is no data. Missing labels do not prove a photo is real.

Free: analysis, metadata viewer, privacy score, GPS Privacy Shield, cleaning and editing up to 75 photos per day,
history of 10 reports. Pro (one-time purchase, no subscription): no daily limit, batch cleaning of up to 50 photos,
PDF and JSON export, history of 50 reports, sending a report to your own API.

App privacy:
• Analysis runs on your device. Images are never uploaded.
• The map (MapTiler, OpenStreetMap data) turns on only when you press its button; until then no map requests are made. Once on, the map provider sees your IP address and where the point is on the map.
• No accounts, ads or analytics.
• Sending a JSON report to your own server is off by default and works only on your command.
